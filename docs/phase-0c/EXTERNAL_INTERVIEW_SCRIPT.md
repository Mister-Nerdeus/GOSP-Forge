# Phase-0C Structured External Interview Script

## Opening

Explain that this is problem discovery, not a claim of certification, professional approval, or a finished product. Ask permission to take structured notes and record any confidentiality constraints. Do not promise follow-up, a pilot, payment terms, or public attribution without separate authorization.

## Evidence and reproducibility pain

1. Which engineering evidence is hardest to preserve or reproduce today?
2. Where do requirements, assumptions, models, tests, and decisions become disconnected?
3. Describe the last time someone could not reproduce or confidently review a result. What was the impact?
4. Which current tools, documents, and handoffs are involved?

## Workflow usefulness

5. Would a structured Challenge / Submission / Evaluation / Evidence package remove work or reduce risk? Which step would matter most?
6. What must be visible before you trust a computed result?
7. Would controlled comparison—showing changed versus fixed inputs and result deltas—improve an existing decision?

## Trust, privacy, and rejection criteria

8. What professional, legal, regulatory, safety, or review boundaries must remain explicit?
9. What information must remain private, and who may access it?
10. What would make you reject this workflow even if the software worked as described?

## Pilot and willingness to pay

11. What real, bounded problem could be used as a pilot without exposing unacceptable risk or confidential data?
12. Who would own the problem, review the result, and decide whether the pilot created value?
13. Would the organization pay to reduce rework, repeated analysis, evidence reconstruction, review preparation, configuration errors, or handoff friction? What buying condition would have to be met?
14. What is the smallest paid discovery or pilot that would be credible?

## Close

Restate objections and requested capabilities in the participant's language. Ask whether follow-up is welcome. Record uncertainty and negative feedback. Do not treat interest, a meeting, or positive language as a design-partner confirmation or paid-pilot commitment.
